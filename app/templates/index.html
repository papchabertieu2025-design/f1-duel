    One model or two?

    At Monaco nobody can overtake, so should those tracks get their own model? We trained two logistic regressions with the same predictors: one on normal tracks, one on the tracks where overtaking is hardest. Each gets its own weights θ.

    How the model is built

    Five steps, one method from the course for each, every step feeding the next.

    1. 1

      Official results

      Every Grand Prix since 2010: 7,231 starts. Only information known before the race is used.

    2. 2

      Linear regression

      One grid place is worth about 0.65 finishing places, and the front rows count more.

    3. 3

      PCA

      Seven team statistics become two axes: car performance and reliability.

    4. 4

      k-means

      Cars fall into three families: top, midfield and back of the grid.

    5. 5

      Logistic regression

      Turns everything into probabilities: points, duels, and the winner (softmax).
